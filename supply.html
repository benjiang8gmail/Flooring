Choosing a timber

How hard is each Australian hardwood?

Harder timbers resist dents from heels, pets and dropped pans. Spotted Gum is one of the hardest common flooring species. Tasmanian Oak and Victorian Ash are softer, but pale, even in colour and very stable.

Hardness isn't everything: colour, board width and finish matter just as much. Bring a sample home and look at it in your own light.

Everything that goes with it

Underlay, trims and accessories

The small parts decide how a floor sounds, how it wears at the edges and whether the warranty holds. We stock the ones each product is tested with.

Underlay

Foam with built-in moisture barrier for slabs, rubber and acoustic underlays for apartments and upstairs rooms.

Trims & transitions

T-moulds, reducers, end caps and stair nosings, colour-matched to the floor in timber, vinyl or aluminium.

Skirting & scotia

Primed MDF skirting in square-edge, pencil-round, bullnose and colonial profiles, plus matching scotia and quad.

Adhesives & prep

Flooring adhesives for direct-stick timber, liquid moisture barriers, primers and self-levelling compound.

Tools for DIY

Tapping blocks, pull bars, expansion spacers and underlay tape. Ask us how to use them.

Care & repair

Cleaning kits, maintenance oils, felt pads and colour-matched filler to keep the floor looking right.

Order quantity

How much flooring to order

Flooring is sold by the box, and every job loses some to cuts at walls and doorways. A straight lay usually needs about 7% extra; diagonal and herringbone layouts need more.

Box coverage is printed on the product label. We'll double-check your numbers before we take an order.
