Floors we supply and lay

Six kinds of hard floor, each with its own job

The right floor depends on the subfloor, the room and how you live. At the measure we'll tell you plainly which of these suits your home and why.

Engineered timber

A real hardwood top layer bonded to a stable core. Looks and feels like solid timber, moves less, and can go straight onto a slab.

Boards
130–220 mm wide, 14–21 mm thick
Laid
Floated, or glued to slab
Suits
Living areas, apartments, slab homes

Solid timber

19 mm tongue-and-groove Australian hardwood, nailed or glued down, then sanded and coated on site. It can be re-sanded many times over its life.

Boards
80–180 mm wide, 19 mm thick
Species
Spotted Gum, Blackbutt, Tas Oak, Vic Ash
Suits
Period homes, long-term owners

Hybrid

A rigid stone-polymer core with a printed timber look and a tough wear layer. Water resistant, quick to lay, and quiet with attached underlay.

Boards
180–230 mm wide, 6.5–9 mm thick
Wear layer
0.3–0.55 mm
Suits
Kitchens, families with pets, rentals

Laminate

A high-density fibreboard core under a printed, hard-wearing surface. The lowest cost per square metre for a timber look in dry areas.

Boards
8–12 mm thick
Rating
AC3 to AC5 abrasion class
Suits
Bedrooms, living areas, tight budgets

Vinyl plank

Thin, flexible planks glued to a smooth subfloor or clicked together. Waterproof and quiet underfoot, and it wraps neatly onto stairs.

Boards
2–3 mm glue-down, 4–5 mm click
Wear layer
0.3–0.7 mm
Suits
Laundries, stairs, shops and offices

Herringbone & chevron

Short engineered or hybrid boards laid in a pattern, wall to wall or inside a border. It takes longer to set out and lay, and the result is worth it.

Boards
70–150 mm wide, 400–750 mm long
Offcut
Allow 10–15% extra
Suits
Entries, living rooms, statement spaces

Under the floor

Most floor problems start underneath

Peaking joints, hollow spots and squeaks almost always trace back to a damp slab or an uneven subfloor. We check both before a single board goes down, and we put the prep on the quote so there are no surprises on the day.

  • Carpet, tiles, vinyl and old floating floors removed and taken away
  • Concrete ground and self-levelled where it's out of tolerance
  • Squeaky boards re-fixed and damaged particleboard replaced
  • Door jambs undercut and doors trimmed to clear the new floor
  • Skirting, scotia, trims and stair nosings to finish every edge

What we check before we lay

  • Slab moistureevery job New slabs can take months to dry. We test before laying and fit a moisture barrier when the product calls for one.
  • Flatness≤ 3 mm / 3 m A common requirement for floating floors, and some products ask for less. High spots are ground, hollows are levelled.
  • Expansion gap10–15 mm Left at every wall and fixed edge so the floor can move with the seasons, then hidden under skirting or scotia.
  • Acclimatisationper maker Timber sits in your home before laying whenever the manufacturer asks for it.

Questions

What people ask before they book

Can't see your question? Call 0491 570 156 or add it to your quote request.

How much does it cost to supply and install a floor in Melbourne?

It depends on the product and how much prep the subfloor needs. As a guide, hybrid and laminate usually land between $55 and $110 per m² supplied and laid, engineered timber between $110 and $190, and solid timber sanded and coated on site between $160 and $260.

Our quote page gives an indicative range for your area. Your fixed price comes after the free measure.

How long does installation take?

For a typical 60 m² job, hybrid or laminate takes one to two days and engineered timber two to three. Solid timber takes four to seven days including sanding and coating, plus curing time before furniture goes back. Herringbone takes longer than a straight lay.

Can you lay a new floor over my existing tiles?

Often, yes, for floating floors. The tiles must be firmly bonded and flat, so we tap-test for drummy tiles and check the extra height at doorways, stairs and appliances. If the tiles aren't sound, we remove them.

Do you remove the old carpet or tiles?

Yes. Removal and disposal is listed as its own line on your quote, and we take everything away.

Can I buy flooring from you and lay it myself?

Yes. Visit the showroom or borrow samples, and we'll work out quantities with the right cutting allowance. We also sell the underlay, trims, adhesives and tools to go with it. See flooring supply.

Will you install flooring I bought somewhere else?

Yes. We check the product suits the room and subfloor first. The product warranty stays with the brand you bought from, and our workmanship is covered by us.

Do you work in apartments?

Yes. Many owners corporations set an acoustic rating your new floor must meet. We'll supply an underlay system tested to that rating, give you the paperwork for the building manager, and work within the building's lift bookings and quiet hours.

Do I need to move my furniture?

Please clear small items, fragile pieces and anything inside cupboards. We can move larger furniture by arrangement; mention it at the measure so it's on the quote.
